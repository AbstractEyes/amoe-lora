How much control?

STANDARD INDUSTRY CURRENT LORAS

These are the loras that almost everyone currently uses in the industry, which includes LORA, LECO, LOHA, and all of those derivatives. They are singular models trained for a specific task or a series of tasks, depending on the necessary goals. Most often a single character, a single style, or something along those lines for diffusion.

PROS





Trains and is done.



Stuck in place, stable, often disturbs standard elements.



Can adjust alpha and contribution.



Some tools available for interpolation and integration.



Multiple variants exist for singular-model LORA structures to enhance/reduce certain blocks or features.



Adds details or adds negative details.



Trains quicker than a full finetune.



Structurally convenient and small so packaging and distribution is easy



Rapid iteration and quick proof of concept for certain behaviors



Solid representations for test cases and distillation utilization

CONS





Stacking loras is a nightmare with a nightmare hat.



Often completely overrides natural model behavior instead of improving it, causing strange quirks and conflicts.



Completely destroys alternative lora behavior rather than augmenting them more often than not, alpha helps but is no guaranteed toggle.



Frozen sigma and timestep gating at training time. You get what you trained.



Multiple loras may introduce unwanted and unexpected elements.



Very difficult to continue finetuning



Difficult to expand or change the scale of



Difficult to extract if a merged lora is tacked to a model.



Tokens bleed over to other tokens regularly



Destructive behavior is uncontrolled and often devastating



Sizes vary wildly and the influence varies wildly with them.



Conflicting tokens cause cascade faults when generic words are trained in contrast.



Often breaks language sequential behavior and turns models into bag of tokens.

AMOE-LORA

An aleph-anchored mixture of experts lora that consists of multiple interconnected independent miniature expert loras directly. A cooperative collective built for the sole purpose of rapid finetuning without losing what you've already trained.

By design you add, modify, or remove elements. 

PROS





Each expert can be trained, added, or removed manually without disrupting the others. This enables many potential features that simply do not exist in current models at all.



You can distill a current lora into an expert and apply a custom token to communicate with only it directly, while the token is not present the expert will not be activated.



Anchors to what a model knows already FIRST in order to improve through generalization. This allows the model to understand the utilization of information and pathways, rather than guessing and twisting numbers.



Experts can be enabled or disabled manually, while the AMOE-LORA automatically gates to the MOE structure so the LORA itself can make it's own intelligent decisions based on which experts are needed for which.



Granular training control, which includes control of experts from tag frequency, to tag strength, to sigma controllers, to distillation elements for speed, to regularization behavior for retention, and anything else an expert will require to contribute. Everything included will be based on transposed SOTA LLM trained systems, ported specifically into the diffusion structure's archaic design and brought up to modern standards.



Integrated distillation elements allowing the AMOE loras to produce faster results intentionally while in use.



Multiple methodologies of caching that allow faster inference, skipping steps, and creating rapid pathological lookups.



Gated tokens and rationalization behind why the gating occurs is a series of tests that passed the bar, that's why the gates exist. These experts are meant to NOT bleed tokens by default, but they will still have some quirks at first until the system refines.



Direct sigma and schedule control training, which allows the AMOE-LORA to control, learn from, and improve the stepping capacity of the diffusion model in question. Each expert may have it's own expertise and the gates will decide which is most suited for the next prediction.



Heavy support for text encoders with the diffusion model being more experimental, the structure is compliant.



Multiple targets of different text encoders that the model itself never natively learned, being capable of introducing additional conditioning into the results. Thus allowing the potential of additional conditioning atop the original conditioning effects, experimentally including interpolation and additive intervals.

CONS





HIGHLY experimental. There WILL be problems, lots of them. HOWEVER, many of the problems are already identified and the reasonable concerns being addressed during packaging. The majority of the core identified problems are resolved, but there will be more.



More training. You will definitely be training more to get the perfect collective. The removal/additive elementals will mitigate this, and the packaged system will include multiple predistilled variants for a few models - expanded over time.



Uncertain the overhead of using many so far, however the MOE behavior is designed to only need a certain amount of experts active per step by default, and the sigma schedule does manage this effectively. That being said the GAIN structure did not hold the way I wanted it to hold, and the logistics behind that means degraded performance until I find a solution.



Granular faults, you will not always know which tokens are causing which faults unless you perform a series of tests. This can complicate processes. I've built the effectiveness of turning on/off manually for strange behavior as well, which will make it easier to debug. The diagnostic system is being constructed to need, so over time this need will present more requirements - however this is heavily adaptive responses. I don't know what many of these systems will do.



Unexpected behavior. There's literally no guarantee this will work beyond the test cases, as the test cases were the testbed. However, sticking with what did work will definitely yield results. Straying too far will yield strange results, negative results, or possibly complete corruption of mini-experts as you train them. Be cautious, this is highly experimental.

I can't say much for the cons, because I've quite literally built this system to be better than standard lora in EVERY WAY I COULD THINK.
